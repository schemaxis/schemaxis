# ParaView portrait perspective framing — 2026-10-25

Changed scope: the default camera distance of perspective (`parallel: false`) image
cameras that do not author `distance` (`publication.py`), the receipt marker
`cameraFramingVersion` (`native_run.py`) and its verification (`job.py`). No new native
option, no declaration or schema change, and the shared `camera.py` is untouched. This note
covers only that scope; it is not a native qualification and does not advance the absorbed
ParaView version (5.13.3; the latest upstream release, 6.2.0, was not absorbed or tested).
The route's `qualification.json` source digest is stale and was not regenerated.

## What was found

ParaView's `ViewAngle` is the vertical angle. The default perspective distance was twice
the bounds diagonal regardless of image shape, so for portrait images (width below height)
the narrower horizontal field of view cropped the object. The parallel branch already
multiplied its scale by height ÷ width.

Native baseline (ParaView 5.13.3, serial `pvbatch`, private profile, 120 s deadline,
unit cube, direction `[1,-1,1]`, `parallel: false`; corner projection read from the live
VTK camera, plus the drawn-pixel bounding box of the decoded PNG):

| Image size | Before max abs ndc (x, y) | Drawn pixels touch edge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 640×480 | 0.623, 0.960 | no |
| 480×480 | 0.831, 0.960 | no |
| 480×640 | 1.108, 0.960 | yes |
| 360×640 | 1.477, 0.960 | yes |

## Change

`distance = 2 × diagonal × max(1, height ÷ width)` for perspective cameras without an
authored `distance`. The visible half-width at distance d is d·tan(angle ÷ 2)·width ÷
height, so the factor makes the portrait horizontal extent equal the vertical extent the
landscape default already used, for any view angle. Landscape and square frames,
parallel projection, an authored `distance`, `scale` and `padding` behave as before.
Padding has never affected the perspective distance and still does not; the landscape
margin (0.96 vertical for a cube) is therefore unchanged.

The clipping range and normalised depth of every corner stayed inside the frustum. The
before and after portrait images were viewed: the cube is cut off at both sides before and
fully visible after. This is a tiny serial probe of one convex solid, not exhaustive
native coverage; MPI and other runtimes are not covered.

## Registry run and receipt checks

A fresh registry `run` of a sphere with portrait (360×640) and landscape (640×360)
perspective images succeeded in serial 5.13.3; the receipt carries
`cameraFramingVersion: 1`, `verify` passed, the portrait image shows the whole sphere,
and a copy whose recorded camera position was moved by 1 unit was refused with
`VERIFICATION_FAILED` ("default perspective camera distance differs from the
aspect-aware framing"). The same receipt with the marker removed (an unversioned receipt)
still verified.

## Receipt compatibility

Receipts bind the authored `effectiveCamera`, not the computed position, so retained
receipts still verify unchanged and are not re-planned. New receipts carry
`cameraFramingVersion: 1`; for those only, verification recomputes the default distance
from the recorded scene bounds and rejects a missing record or a different distance
(including the old cropping distance). Re-running a portrait perspective scene now gives a
different, uncropped image.

## Host checks

`test_paraview_publication_framing.py`: 11 tests (distance formula for landscape, square,
portrait and parallel; independent pinhole projection showing the old distance crops and
the new one does not across sizes, directions and bounds; frame placement with explicit
distance, parallel, padding and scale; receipt verification for version 1, tampered,
missing, historical-distance, unversioned and explicit-distance cases). The guidance audit
passes with no stale records. `readiness --app paraview --admission --check` still reports
`structurallyComplete: false` for the route; this work does not claim to close those gaps.

## Not claimed

Native qualification of ParaView 6.2.0, MPI behaviour, perspective padding, or any other
camera behaviour.
