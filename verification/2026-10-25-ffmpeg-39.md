# FFmpeg review-movie verification — 25 October 2026

A bounded `schemaxis.ffmpeg/1` `muxReview` operation combines a captured silent
Matroska/FFV1 video, PCM16 WAV and plain SubRip captions into a three-stream
Matroska file. FFmpeg/ffprobe 9.0.2 were exercised on Linux. The existing
`encodeFrames`, `encodeSheet` and `encodeVideo` operations keep their previous
verification policy; this record does not upgrade their retained-only evidence.

## Contract and native mapping

- One FFV1 `bgr0` video, mono/stereo PCM16 audio at 8000–96000 Hz, and plain UTF-8
  SubRip; explicit order and stream maps, fixed stream-copy commands, no authored
  native flags, network inputs, attachments, chapters or extra streams.
- The existing video limits apply: 1–600 frames, integer constant frame rate,
  bounded dimensions and decoded RGB. Audio is bounded to 32 MiB; captions to
  256 KiB and 1000 cues. See `docs/ffmpeg.md` for the complete subset.
- Audio and video nominal durations may differ by at most one video-frame period,
  judged by exact rational arithmetic. Every caption must end within the video.
  No trimming, padding, resampling, burn-in or caption retiming is performed.
- Captions have the explicit `machineDraft` status. Recognition accuracy and
  human review are not verified or certified.

## What verification proves

The verifier opens the actual retained publication again using a trusted FFmpeg
and its sibling ffprobe. Runtime selection comes from an explicit library
argument, trusted caller configuration, or `FFMPEG_EXECUTABLE`; recorded commands
and receipt executable paths are inert data. Standalone verification does not
search `PATH`. A run may initially resolve its executable from `PATH` and passes
that caller-selected executable to its own verification.

All three captured inputs and the published movie are held during the decision.
Fixed subprocesses in a separate temporary directory reopen those exact bytes.
The host compares every decoded video frame with the captured FFV1 source, every
PCM byte with the captured WAV, and all extracted cue text and millisecond
intervals with the captured SRT. Exact three-stream membership, supported codec
metadata, absent extra metadata and source-derived facts are checked. Fresh
facts and digests must agree with retained observations, receipt and result;
retained JSON is strict and comparisons preserve numeric/boolean types.

Video and audio start at zero. Every published video timestamp follows its
constant-rate frame clock within Matroska's 1.1 ms rounding bound. A separate
bounded audio-packet probe derives sample-frame counts from PCM16 packet sizes:
packet counts must sum to the captured sample count and every packet must follow
the cumulative sample clock within the same rounding bound. Packet duration
metadata, when present, is checked too. This detects internal audio gaps and
overlaps even when the start time, PCM bytes and coarse container-duration policy
remain plausible. Fresh video probes are limited to 600 frames; audio probes to
8 MiB and 32768 packets.

A fresh-pass refusal is tagged `observed.fresh: true`. Timeout, cancellation and
missing trusted-runtime faults retain their own structured codes. Temporary
verification directories are removed. A failed publication removes an unverified
review movie where possible and retains failure evidence.

The captured inputs are the trust anchors, not authenticated copies of the
original authored paths. A coherent replacement of captures, publication and all
evidence is not detected as an authenticity violation. Equivalent container
changes that preserve every checked decoded sample, cue, timestamp and supported
metadata can verify after consistent evidence refresh. This is not a signed
history or a claim of byte-for-byte remux identity.

- The native qualifier passed 79 probes, including ordinary and changed inputs,
  stereo 48 kHz audio, CRLF captions, exact duration boundaries, extra-stream and
  unsupported-format refusals, missing resources, lifecycle and incomplete runs.
- Fully refreshed caption/PCM forgeries, retained frame-count/dimension forgeries,
  audio/video offsets and internal audio gaps/overlaps were refused by the fresh

  facts probes. Positive 24/30 fps clips with a nonzero first cue passed.

- 92 affected host checks passed. Full-suite integration is recorded separately

- Native adaptor conformance passed with an explicit `--executable`, an empty

  caller locations through execution, verification and integrity checks. A bare
  `schemaxis verify` still needs a stored configuration or environment runtime.
- A four-job native handoff combined a waveform PNG into 110 FFV1 frames at 10 fps,
  prepared an 11-second stereo 44.1 kHz recording with SoX, transcribed it with
  whisper.cpp and muxed its SRT, prepared PCM and video. The existing run verified
  read-only as four jobs and nine artifacts after a shared object-key-order
  provenance fix. This is one tested clip, not general recognition, human-review,
  accessibility, perceptual or browser-playback certification.

## Limits

Only the named runtime and bounded cases were exercised. A midstream video shift
with zero initial offset is unit-tested; the native remux attempts reached the
start-time guard first. Sub-millisecond timing differences inside the declared
1.1 ms rounding bound are accepted. No MPI, AAC, H.264 review-movie delivery,
WebVTT input, styled captions, original-path authenticity or recognition-quality
certification is claimed. ASR-derived words and caption suitability still need
human review.

## Native readback boundary: 96 kHz extensible WAV

After the first acceptance, positive probes at 8, 16, 22.05 and 48 kHz with odd
final samples passed, but a 96 kHz source (a canonical, admitted WAV) failed
verification of its own native output: FFmpeg writes the PCM16 readback above
48 kHz with a 40-byte `WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE` `fmt ` chunk, and the shared parser
accepted only the 16-byte form. The source contract is unchanged. The readback
is now read by a dedicated strict parser that also accepts exactly that header
(tag 65534, 22 extension bytes, 16 valid bits, integer-PCM GUID, front-centre mask
for mono and front-left/right for stereo, consistent alignment and byte rate,
`data` directly after `fmt `, exact RIFF and data sizes). Other GUIDs, valid
bits, layouts, masks, chunks, truncation and trailing bytes are verification
faults; the decoded PCM still has to equal the captured PCM. Sources above 96 kHz
are still `UNSUPPORTED_AUDIO`; the earlier 192 kHz probe was that limit, not a defect.
The native decode command, retained receipt format and genuine 16/48 kHz readbacks
are unchanged.

Checks run for this repair (new, FFmpeg/ffprobe 9.0.2, serial):

- The native review qualifier passed 80 probes (the earlier 79 plus a 192 kHz
  refusal as `UNSUPPORTED_AUDIO` without publication). It now also runs native
  mux, verification and an independent literal-PCM comparison at 8, 16, 22.05 and
  48 kHz mono, 48 kHz stereo and 96 kHz mono and stereo, each with an odd final
  sample count; the readback `fmt ` chunk was 16 bytes through 48 kHz and 40
  bytes at 96 kHz.
- Unit tests cover the captured 96 kHz mono header, mono/stereo extensible
  readbacks including odd final samples, canonical readbacks at 8/16/22.05/48 kHz,
  and refusal of fmt-40 source WAVs. Refused as verification faults: float and zeroed
  GUIDs, wrong tag, valid bits 8/15/24, wrong extension size, 24- and 32-bit,
  wrong or zero channel masks, three or zero channels, rates outside 8–96 kHz,
  byte rate or alignment errors, other `fmt ` sizes, `fact`/`LIST` chunks, empty
  or partial-frame data, truncation, trailing bytes and RIFF/data size
  disagreement; changed PCM, another rate or another length also fail the decoded-PCM
  comparison. The 60 FFmpeg and media host tests passed; the full suite was not run.
- Eight earlier accepted retained runs (8, 16, 22.05 and 48 kHz from two probe
  batches) verified read-only with the repaired code, with every retained file
  byte-identical afterwards.

Limits: one FFmpeg/ffprobe 9.0.2 build and one bounded clip, serial. No other
sample format, layout or rate is claimed. The 24-byte edge limit that this repair
first recorded (a 96 kHz source within 24 bytes of the 32 MiB budget refused at
readback) is resolved below.

## Readback budget and layout rule

A canonical 96 kHz mono source of exactly 32 MiB was accepted as input, but its
native readback is 33554456 bytes (the 40-byte `fmt ` adds 24), and the readback
size bound of 32 MiB refused it as `VERIFICATION_FAILED`. The retained before-run
showed exactly that. The repair adds an internal read bound of 32 MiB plus 24 bytes
for `decoded-audio.wav` only; source, input and PCM budgets, the native command
(`-fs` 32 MiB + 1) and the launcher limits (4 MiB of slack) are unchanged. The
exact-budget native probe was observed to write the complete valid file under this
unchanged `-fs`; any readback cut short would fail the exact PCM comparison. The native parser now also requires the layout FFmpeg 9.0.2 was observed
to write: `fmt ` of 40 bytes if and only if the rate is above 48 kHz; a 48 kHz
extensible or a 96 kHz canonical readback header is refused.

Checks run for this repair (new, FFmpeg/ffprobe 9.0.2, serial):

- A fresh native run on a new output directory: an exact 32 MiB, 96 kHz mono WAV
  with a 175-frame, 1 fps FFV1 video, captions and the same unmodified command was
  verified, with a 33554456-byte readback whose `fmt ` is 40 bytes and whose PCM
  equals the source PCM byte for byte. A standalone `verify` of that run, including
  its fresh native decode, also passed. A source of 32 MiB plus 2 bytes was still
  refused as `RESOURCE_LIMIT` without publication, and a copy of the run whose
  readback had one byte more than 32 MiB plus 24 was refused as
  `VERIFICATION_FAILED` (oversized readback).
- Sixteen earlier or new retained runs (8, 16, 22.05 and 48 kHz from several batches,
  48 kHz stereo, 96 kHz mono and stereo, and the new run) verified read-only with the
  repaired code, every retained file byte-identical afterwards. The 96 kHz run in one
  earlier batch had no verification record and was not included.
- Unit tests add the two impossible-layout negatives (48 kHz extensible and 96 kHz
  canonical, plus a 16 kHz stereo and a 64 kHz stereo variant), the 48000/48001 Hz
  layout boundary, and the exact internal read bound at 32 MiB plus 24 and plus 25
  bytes. The FFmpeg unit files (62 tests) and the media tests (30, one skipped) passed;
  the full host suite and the 80-probe native qualifier were not re-run for this
  change. The qualifier helper `full_refresh` now uses the native parser; it was
  only compile-checked.
