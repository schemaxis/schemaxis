## box

Creates a MeshInstance3D holding a native BoxMesh centred on position, with edge lengths size in metres and the connected material. Choose it for blocks, walls, platforms or bounding volumes; it is the simplest primitive and the box readback (12 triangles, exact bounds) is the easiest to check. The mesh stays an editable BoxMesh in Godot. Use plane for a zero-thickness floor and readGLTF for shapes that are not primitives.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- size holds full edge lengths, not half-extents: size [2, 2, 2] spans -1 to 1 around position.
- It needs a material input naming an earlier material node. Schemaxis qualified this operation natively only on small CPU-only scenes with Godot 4.7.2 (build, fresh-process reopen and readback); visual quality and GUI editing were not qualified.

Native binding: `BoxMesh`. Output kind: `["geometry"]`.

| Option | Meaning | Type / constraints | Default |

| `size` | Full edge lengths [x, y, z] of the box in metres before rotation and scale, written to BoxMesh size. The box extends half of each length on either side of position. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":0.001,"maximum":10000},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[1,1,1]` |
| `rotation` | Orientation of the box as Euler angles [x, y, z] in degrees, written to Godot's rotation_degrees and applied after scale and before the move to position. ## camera

Creates the scene's single perspective Camera3D at position, turned with look_at so that it looks at target with +Y up, and makes it the current camera. Every Godot document needs exactly one camera node; it frames the optional preview image and the view when the saved project is run. With orbit enabled, a fixed trusted script lets a user orbit, zoom and reset the view interactively in Godot.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- A camera directly above or below its target (no horizontal separation) is rejected, because the Y-up look-at direction would be undefined; offset position slightly in X or Z for a near top-down view.
- Schemaxis does not check that geometry is inside the view; a preview can be valid but empty if the camera misses it.
- A second camera node fails planning with INVALID_SELECTION.

Native binding: `Camera3D`. Output kind: `["camera"]`.

Inputs: none.

| Option | Meaning | Type / constraints | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `position` | World position [x, y, z] of the camera eye in metres. Together with target it fixes the view direction; the camera has no separate rotation setting. Its distance from target must be 0.01 m to 100 km and it must not be directly above or below target. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":-100000,"maximum":100000},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[5,4,7]` |
| `target` | World point [x, y, z] in metres that the camera looks at; Schemaxis calls Godot's look_at with +Y as up, so the camera never rolls. With orbit enabled, it is also the pivot the interactive orbit turns around. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":-100000,"maximum":100000},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[0,0,0]` |
| `fov` | Field-of-view angle of the perspective camera in degrees, written to Camera3D fov. With Godot's default aspect mode (keep height) this is the vertical angle; the horizontal angle follows from the window or preview aspect ratio. Smaller values zoom in and flatten perspective; larger values widen the view and exaggerate perspective. | `{"type":"number","minimum":10,"maximum":120}` | `55` |
| `near` | Distance in metres from the camera to the near clipping plane, written to Camera3D near; anything closer is not drawn. It must be smaller than far. With orbit on, it also limits how close the wheel can zoom. | `{"type":"number","minimum":0.001,"maximum":100}` | `0.05` |
| `far` | Distance in metres from the camera to the far clipping plane, written to Camera3D far; anything farther is not drawn. It must be greater than near. | `{"type":"number","minimum":1,"maximum":100000}` | `1000` |
| `orbit` | When true, Schemaxis attaches its fixed, trusted orbit.gd script to the camera: in the running Godot project, left-drag orbits around target, the mouse wheel zooms and R resets the view. When false, the camera is fixed and has no script. It does not change the saved view or the preview image. | `{"type":"boolean"}` | `true` |


## capsule

Creates a MeshInstance3D holding a native CapsuleMesh: a cylinder with hemispherical ends standing along its local Y axis, centred on position. height is the total height including both caps, so it must be at least twice the radius; tessellation is fixed at 32 radial segments and 8 rings. Choose it for character or obstacle placeholders and rounded rods.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- A height below 2 x radius fails planning with INVALID_ARGUMENT; with height exactly 2 x radius the capsule is a sphere.
- It needs a material input naming an earlier material node. Schemaxis qualified this operation natively only on small CPU-only scenes with Godot 4.7.2 (build, fresh-process reopen and readback); visual quality and GUI editing were not qualified.

Native binding: `CapsuleMesh`. Output kind: `["geometry"]`.

## cylinder

Creates a MeshInstance3D holding a native CylinderMesh with equal top and bottom radius, standing along its local Y axis and centred on position (it extends height/2 above and below). Tessellation is fixed at 32 radial segments and one ring (192 triangles). Choose it for posts, pipes and columns; rotate it to lie along another axis. Cones are not available because only one radius is accepted.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- position is the centre of the cylinder, not its base: to stand a 1.2 m post on the ground at y = 0, use position y = 0.6.
- It needs a material input naming an earlier material node. Schemaxis qualified this operation natively only on small CPU-only scenes with Godot 4.7.2 (build, fresh-process reopen and readback); visual quality and GUI editing were not qualified.

## directionalLight

Creates a DirectionalLight3D: parallel light from a distant source such as the sun, whose direction comes only from rotation (light travels along the node's rotated -Z axis); it has no position and lights the whole scene equally. Choose it as the main key or sun light; add omniLight or spotLight for local light. Shadows are not offered for this operation. Schemaxis does not require any light node; add one when the preview or running project should show directional shading.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- Rotation [0, 0, 0] points the light horizontally along -Z; tilt it downward with a negative X angle (the default is [-45, -30, 0]).

Native binding: `DirectionalLight3D`. Output kind: `["light"]`.

Inputs: none.

| Option | Meaning | Type / constraints | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rotation` | Orientation of the light as Euler angles [x, y, z] in degrees (Godot YXZ order), written to rotation_degrees. The light travels along the node's rotated -Z axis: the X angle tilts it up or down (negative is downward) and the Y angle turns its heading about the vertical axis; the Z angle has no visible effect. Moving the node would not matter, so there is no position. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":-180,"maximum":180},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[-45,-30,0]` |
| `colour` | Colour of the light as [red, green, blue], each 0 to 1, written to light_color (Godot interprets it as non-linear sRGB). Lit surfaces are tinted by it; white leaves material colours unchanged. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":1},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[1,1,1]` |
| `energy` | Brightness multiplier of the sunlight, written to light_energy. Godot states it is not a physical unit: 2 is twice as bright as 1 and 0 switches the light off. | `{"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":16}` | `1` |


## material

Creates a Godot StandardMaterial3D resource with an opaque base colour, roughness and metallic value; it is not a scene node by itself. Choose it once per distinct surface look and connect it to the material input of box, sphere, cylinder, plane, capsule, torus or readGLTF nodes; several meshes may share one material. The resource is saved inside scene.tscn as each mesh's material override, and the reopened values are compared with the authored ones.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- Every material must feed at least one geometry node; an unused material fails planning with INVALID_SELECTION.
- Declare the material before the meshes that use it; inputs may only refer to earlier nodes.
- Transparency, textures and emission are not available; alpha is always 1. Schemaxis qualified this operation natively only on small CPU-only scenes with Godot 4.7.2 (build, fresh-process reopen and readback); visual quality and GUI editing were not qualified.

Native binding: `StandardMaterial3D`. Output kind: `["material"]`.

Inputs: none.

| Option | Meaning | Type / constraints | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `colour` | Base (albedo) colour of the surface as [red, green, blue], each from 0 to 1, written to albedo_color with alpha 1. Lit surfaces show this colour modulated by lights; for metallic surfaces it tints the reflections instead of the diffuse colour. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":1},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[0.3,0.6,0.9]` |
| `roughness` | How rough the surface is, from 0 to 1. Godot treats 0 as a perfect mirror and 1 as fully blurred reflection; intermediate values give broader, softer highlights as the value increases. | `{"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":1}` | `0.6` |
| `metallic` | How metal-like the surface is, from 0 (dielectric such as plastic or paint) to 1 (metal). Metals use the base colour to tint reflections and have no diffuse colour; Godot recommends 0 or 1 and intermediate values only for blending between metal and non-metal. | `{"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":1}` | `0` |


## omniLight

Creates an OmniLight3D: a point light at position that shines in all directions and lights nothing beyond range metres. attenuation shapes how brightness falls with distance inside that radius, and shadows can be enabled. Choose it for lamps, fill and rim lights; use spotLight for a directed cone and directionalLight for sunlight.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- Objects farther than range from the light receive none of its light, however high energy is.

Native binding: `OmniLight3D`. Output kind: `["light"]`.

Inputs: none.

| Option | Meaning | Type / constraints | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `position` | World position [x, y, z] of the light source in metres. Light spreads from this point in all directions. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":-100000,"maximum":100000},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[0,0,0]` |
| `colour` | Colour of the light as [red, green, blue], each 0 to 1, written to light_color (non-linear sRGB in Godot). A cool fill light such as [0.6, 0.75, 1] contrasts with a warm key light. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":1},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[1,1,1]` |
| `energy` | Brightness multiplier of the light, written to light_energy; Godot states it is not a physical unit. For omni lights it changes intensity only, not how far the light reaches (that is range). | `{"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":16}` | `1` |
| `range` | Radius in metres of the sphere the light can reach, written to omni_range. Nothing outside this radius receives any of its light, whatever the attenuation; inside it, brightness falls off according to attenuation. | `{"type":"number","minimum":0.001,"maximum":100000}` | `5` |
| `attenuation` | Shape of the brightness fall-off with distance inside range, written to omni_attenuation. Godot describes values near 0 as nearly constant brightness that fades only at the edge of range, and 2 as physically accurate inverse-square fall-off; 1 is in between. | `{"type":"number","minimum":0.001,"maximum":16}` | `1` |
| `shadows` | When true, the light casts real-time shadows (Light3D shadow_enabled), so objects block it from surfaces behind them. Godot notes this has a significant rendering cost. It affects the preview image and the running project, not the verified geometry. | `{"type":"boolean"}` | `false` |


## plane

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- With the default material settings Godot draws only the face pointing along the plane's +Y normal, so a plane seen from below or from behind is invisible; rotate it so its normal faces the camera.
- It needs a material input naming an earlier material node. Schemaxis qualified this operation natively only on small CPU-only scenes with Godot 4.7.2 (build, fresh-process reopen and readback); visual quality and GUI editing were not qualified.

## readGLTF

Imports one static triangle mesh from a self-contained .gltf 2.0 file with Godot's GLTFDocument and places it as an editable ArrayMesh in a MeshInstance3D, using the connected material and the authored position, rotation and scale. Choose it to bring CAD or modelling output into a scene when no primitive fits. Schemaxis accepts only a narrow subset (one scene, one untransformed node, one mesh with one TRIANGLES primitive, embedded base64 buffers, POSITION and optional NORMAL, at most 8 MiB) and rejects anything else instead of discarding it. The file bytes are captured and hashed before import, and the native bounds and triangle count are checked against values measured from those bytes.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- GLB files, external .bin or texture URIs, node transforms, glTF materials, animation, skins and extensions are rejected with UNSUPPORTED_RESOURCE; export a plain embedded .gltf mesh instead.
- The glTF's own materials are not imported; the surface look comes only from the material input.
- Verification establishes bounds and triangle count, not vertex-by-vertex or normal equivalence. Schemaxis qualified this operation natively only on small CPU-only scenes with Godot 4.7.2 (build, fresh-process reopen and readback); visual quality and GUI editing were not qualified.

Native binding: `GLTFDocument`. Output kind: `["geometry"]`.

| Input port | Multiplicity | Required | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `material` | one node | yes | `material` |

| Option | Meaning | Type / constraints | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `position` | World position [x, y, z] in metres where the glTF mesh's own origin is placed. The mesh is not re-centred: if its vertices lie away from their origin, the mesh appears offset from position by the same amount. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":-100000,"maximum":100000},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[0,0,0]` |
| `file` | Absolute path of the .gltf 2.0 file to import. The file must contain one scene with one untransformed node, one mesh with one TRIANGLES primitive, embedded base64 buffers, float POSITION and optional NORMAL, at most 100000 elements per accessor and 8 MiB in size. ## spotLight

Creates a SpotLight3D: a cone of light from position along the node's rotated -Z axis, with angle as the cone's half-angle and range as its reach in metres. attenuation and angleAttenuation shape the fall-off with distance and towards the cone edge; shadows can be enabled. Choose it for key lights, stage lights or torches; use omniLight for light in all directions.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- With rotation [0, 0, 0] the cone points horizontally along -Z; aim it with a negative X angle (downward) and a Y angle (heading).
- angle is measured from the cone axis to its edge, so 45 gives a 90-degree wide cone.

Native binding: `SpotLight3D`. Output kind: `["light"]`.

| `rotation` | Aim of the spot light as Euler angles [x, y, z] in degrees (Godot YXZ order), written to rotation_degrees. The cone axis is the node's rotated -Z axis: the X angle tilts it (negative points downward) and the Y angle sets its heading; the Z angle only rolls the cone about its own axis. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":-180,"maximum":180},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[0,0,0]` |
| `colour` | Colour of the light as [red, green, blue], each 0 to 1, written to light_color (non-linear sRGB in Godot). | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":1},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[1,1,1]` |
| `energy` | Brightness multiplier of the spot light, written to light_energy; Godot states it is not a physical unit. It changes intensity only, not the reach (range) or cone width (angle). | `{"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":16}` | `1` |

## sprite3D

Embeds a PNG image as an editable Sprite3D with an ImageTexture: a flat, double-sided picture centred on position, facing +Z, whose world size is the image size in pixels multiplied by pixelSize. Choose it to show pixel art, a sprite sheet from an Aseprite operation or any other PNG inside a 3D scene; it needs no material and counts as the visible geometry a scene requires. Schemaxis uses nearest-neighbour filtering so pixels stay crisp, and checks the reopened texture's dimensions and RGBA pixel digest.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- Only non-interlaced 8-bit RGB or RGBA PNG up to 4096 x 4096 pixels is accepted; palette, greyscale, 16-bit and interlaced files fail with INVALID_IMAGE.
- The sprite is not a billboard and has no rotation or scale option: it always faces +Z, so a camera looking along +Z sees its back (mirrored).
- A whole sprite sheet is shown as one picture; frames are not animated.

Native binding: `Sprite3D`. Output kind: `["image"]`.

Inputs: none.

| Option | Meaning | Type / constraints | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `position` | World position [x, y, z] in metres of the centre of the sprite rectangle. The picture lies in a plane parallel to XY through this point. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":-100000,"maximum":100000},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[0,0,0]` |
| `file` | Absolute path of the PNG image to show. Schemaxis decodes the actual pixels, copies the file into the run directory and embeds the texture in the saved scene, so the original file may later move. Accepted images are non-interlaced 8-bit RGB or RGBA PNG up to 4096 x 4096 pixels; transparent pixels stay transparent. In a workflow, an image or spriteSheet artifact (for example an Aseprite preview or sheet) can supply this file. | `{"type":"string","minLength":1,"maxLength":4096}` | **required** |
| `pixelSize` | Width and height in metres of one image pixel in the scene, written to Sprite3D pixel_size. The sprite's world size is its pixel dimensions times this value: a 16 x 16 image with pixelSize 0.05 is 0.8 m square. | `{"type":"number","minimum":1e-05,"maximum":10}` | `0.01` |


## torus

Creates a MeshInstance3D holding a native TorusMesh: a ring lying in its local XZ plane around the local Y axis, centred on position. innerRadius is the radius of the hole and outerRadius the overall radius, so the tube is outerRadius - innerRadius thick. Tessellation is fixed at 32 rings and 16 ring segments. Choose it for rings, wheels and seals.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- innerRadius must be smaller than outerRadius; equal or reversed values fail planning with INVALID_ARGUMENT.
- It needs a material input naming an earlier material node. Schemaxis qualified this operation natively only on small CPU-only scenes with Godot 4.7.2 (build, fresh-process reopen and readback); visual quality and GUI editing were not qualified.

Native binding: `TorusMesh`. Output kind: `["geometry"]`.

| Input port | Multiplicity | Required | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `material` | one node | yes | `material` |

| Option | Meaning | Type / constraints | Default |

| `position` | World position [x, y, z] of the torus centre (the centre of the hole) in metres. | `{"type":"array","items":{"type":"number","minimum":-100000,"maximum":100000},"minItems":3,"maxItems":3}` | `[0,0,0]` |
| `innerRadius` | Radius of the hole in metres: the distance from the torus centre to the inner edge of the tube, written to TorusMesh inner_radius. It must be smaller than outerRadius; the tube thickness is outerRadius - innerRadius. | `{"type":"number","minimum":0.001,"maximum":10000}` | `0.5` |
