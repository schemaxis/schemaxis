## equilibrate

For each listed initial temperature, sets the gas to that temperature and its pressure and then calls Cantera's ThermoPhase.equilibrate with the element-potential solver, holding either temperature and pressure (TP) or specific enthalpy and pressure (HP, adiabatic). It publishes the same results.csv columns as thermoSweep, now with the equilibrium composition and, for HP, the adiabatic final temperature, plus chemistry-summary.json with element-conservation and chemical-potential residuals. Choose it for dissociation at fixed temperature or adiabatic flame temperatures of hydrogen mixtures; choose thermoSweep for a non-reacting property table.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- Rows are independent starting states, not a transient trajectory; equilibrium says nothing about how fast the state is reached.
- In HP mode the final temperature must also lie within 300-3500 K; an undiluted hot mixture can exceed it and fail verification, so add N2 or AR or lower the initial temperature.
- Independent verification checks element inventories, NASA7 properties and that every accessible species' chemical potential matches the element potentials within 2e-5 RT.

Native binding: `cantera.ThermoPhase.equilibrate`. Output kind: `["table"]`.

## gas

Defines the starting gas state for the second node: a pressure in pascals and a hydrogen/oxygen/inert mixture drawn from the ten species of Cantera's h2o2.yaml ideal-gas phase ohmech. It produces no table itself; it is always the first of the two nodes, and the thermoSweep or equilibrate node that follows evaluates it at each requested temperature, while a reactorHistory node uses it as the reactor state at time zero. Amounts are proportions on the chosen mole or mass basis and are normalised, so they need not sum to one. Choose another tool when the mixture contains carbon, NOx or condensed phases; this mechanism cannot represent them.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- Each species may appear only once and the amounts must have a positive total, otherwise planning fails with INVALID_COMPOSITION.
- N2 and AR are inert in this mechanism: no nitrogen oxides or argon compounds ever form, even at high temperature.
- The gas is ideal; no real-gas correction is applied, so states near condensation or at the upper pressure limit are idealised.

Native binding: `cantera.Solution`. Output kind: `["gas"]`.

Inputs: none.

## reactorHistory

Integrates one closed homogeneous ideal-gas reactor in time from the gas node state at the chosen initial temperature, using Cantera's IdealGasConstPressureReactor (pressure held) or IdealGasReactor (volume held) in a ReactorNet with the 29-reaction h2o2 mechanism. It publishes results.csv with one row per requested sample time (time, temperature, pressure, density, energies, heat capacities, mole and mass fractions) and chemistry-summary.json with the reactor model, solver settings read back from Cantera, the ignition delay by the declared temperature-rise criterion, conservation residuals and the verification tolerances. Choose it to see how fast a hydrogen mixture reacts, for example an ignition delay or cooling under a fixed-temperature bath; choose equilibrate when only the final equilibrium state matters. Optional thermalConductance and bathTemperature connect a fixed bath through a linear heat-transfer wall; zero conductance preserves the adiabatic model. The bath exchanges heat only and never gas. At constant pressure, volume changes to maintain pressure while mass stays fixed.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- The reactor is closed. Optional fixed-bath exchange uses total conductance W/K, independent of instantaneous volume; no moving/radiating wall, wall heat capacity, inflow, mixing or transport is modelled. Results describe this model, not a measurement.
- The ignition delay is defined only by ignitionTemperatureRise (first time T reaches initial T plus the rise, interpolated linearly between native integrator steps); other definitions, such as the peak of OH or of dT/dt, can give different values.
- Samples are not interpolated between rows: put enough sample times around the expected ignition to see the temperature rise, and note that the CSV alone does not show the shape between samples.
- Verification integrates the same mechanism independently in host Python; this checks the native result against the stated model, not the model against experiments.
- With a hot bath, external heating can cross the temperature-rise ignition criterion even without chemistry. Interpret delay together with composition and heat balance; it does not prove autoignition.

Native binding: `cantera.ReactorNet`. Output kind: `["table"]`.

## thermoSweep

Evaluates the gas at each listed temperature with its composition and pressure frozen, publishing results.csv (one row per temperature: density, specific enthalpy, internal energy, entropy, cp, cv, mean molecular weight and the unchanged mole and mass fractions) plus chemistry-summary.json. Choose it for property tables of a non-reacting mixture, for example heat capacity of a diluent or density of a fixed fuel/air mixture; choose equilibrate when the composition should be allowed to react to chemical equilibrium. The host recomputes every property from the public NASA7 coefficients and a second Cantera process reloads the saved states before the run is accepted.

Common mistakes and assumptions:

- No reaction occurs: a hydrogen/oxygen mixture listed at 3000 K is reported unreacted, which is physically unstable; use equilibrate for reacting states.
- Rows are independent states, not a heating process; their order carries no history.
- Keep chemistry-summary.json with the CSV; the CSV alone does not record the mechanism, basis or units.

Native binding: `cantera.ThermoPhase`. Output kind: `["table"]`.
